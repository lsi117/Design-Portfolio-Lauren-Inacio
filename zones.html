The challenge

A patient-safety problem wearing an enablement costume

Through the Integrated Multivendor Services partnership, IBM and Zones brought stronger security solutions to a notoriously under-resourced buyer: small and mid-tier hospitals. But a great solution only matters if the person selling it understands it, and when a seller guides a hospital security decision, the downstream effect lands on doctors and patients.

How might we help busy sellers learn security technologies in the gaps of their day, and feel genuinely ready to advise a hospital, without adding to their cognitive load?

The person

Tim, the technology seller

Ten years in the industry and genuinely expert, Tim handles multiple accounts and recommends technology that fits each client. He has almost no time to learn a new technology before it reaches clients, prefers hands-on training he rarely gets, and struggles to keep up with the unique environment of every hospital he advises.

The hill: Tim will have a centralized, easy-to-navigate portal that equips him to confidently sell security technologies to small and mid-tier hospitals.

How I worked

Start with the people

I ran interviews with Zones sellers to map the as-is experience: their workflow, their frustrations, the moments they felt exposed in front of a client. Those conversations are why the product solved the sellers real problem rather than the one I had assumed. What emerged was a minimal delightful experience: modular lessons in digestible chunks, inline knowledge checks after each lesson, and an iterative loop of testing with the sellers themselves.

Revisiting it

Redesigned as a system

Returning to my first IBM project years later, the content strategy and research held up. What was missing was systems thinking: the original was a set of well-designed screens, not yet a system. I rebuilt it on IBM Carbon, token-based, documenting every deliberate departure as a parity map: teal promoted to a dedicated learning-progress accent so IBM blue keeps meaning action; softened corners and two subtle elevation levels so a learning product reads as approachable; larger body type in service of the digestible principle.

Knowing the rules well enough to break them precisely is the part of this I am proudest of.

Accessibility and words

Inclusive by default, not retrofitted

  • WCAG 2.1 AA as the floor: visible focus states on every control, labelled inputs throughout.
  • Quiz and status meaning carried by colour and icon and text, critical given how much green and red the quizzes use.
  • Sentence case with plain, active verbs (check answer, continue, mark complete), consistent through each flow.
  • Empty and error states that give direction instead of apology.

A note on scope: the original portal was designed, shipped, and validated with Zones sellers. The redesign is a self-initiated exploration; I keep the two clearly separated.
