How I work

A loose double diamond (diverge to understand, converge to ship), adapted to enterprise reality: deep technical domains, distributed teams, and users you have to earn access to.

01

Understand the domain first

I sit with the code, the data, and the people who live in them. Interviews, workflow analysis, and my own developer background: the shape of the problem comes before any screen.

02

Frame the real problem

Synthesis into personas, journey maps, and a problem statement the whole team signs up to, so we argue about the user’s problem, not each other’s opinions.

03

Prototype, test, repeat

Low fidelity until the direction survives contact with real users. Usability testing and iteration do the deciding, not the loudest voice in the room.

04

Ship with engineers, then learn

Specs, tokens, and patterns engineers actually use; daily contact with distributed teams. After release, telemetry and support tickets feed the next diamond.

About

Users are never just one persona. They might be a developer, an executive assistant, or even you.

I'm a Product Designer at IBM, where I create enterprise AI and application analysis experiences for complex B2B software. Most of my work comes down to the same thing: simplifying technical workflows and turning complex data into product experiences that feel intuitive.

I started in software development and switched to design in 2019, which means I design developer tools as someone who's actually shipped code. That keeps me close to the people I design for and comfortable in technical, ambiguous problem spaces. Since then I've built design systems, led design on enterprise products like z/OS Cloud Broker, and helped engineering and design teams work more closely.
